Original Warscythe manuscript. 48 collected records, ordered here by editorial thread. The app uses a separate nonchronological discovery sequence. Dates are dates of writing; papers are encountered as retained originals or attributed copies.

## chain-01 — An alteration to a smith's estimate

Iven · 3 Longrain, 642 · notice

Joric—

The estimate was eight silver marks when I made it. It is twenty-three now. No, that is not for a pair.

Ashenveil has sent neither iron nor word in seventeen days. The merchant wants payment before he will tell me whether his next wagon contains metal. The charcoal man has discovered that fear burns as well as wood, and charges accordingly.

Your deposit still stands. If you cannot afford the spear, bring your father's. I can set a new socket around the old head.

Tell your boy to stop striking stones with it. I cannot forge him a second inheritance.

Iven

## chain-02 — Queen Nerys to the treasury at Aurelius Keep

Queen Nerys · 7 Longrain, 642 · letter

To the Keeper of the Treasury,

Your last payment bore the correct face and the wrong weight. I have enclosed three of the coins. A portrait cannot make up the missing silver.

We have opened the western cistern to your refugees. We will keep it open. But a bucket must be made somewhere, a mule must eat something, and the men repairing the sluice will not take royal assurances home for supper.

Send grain, sound metal, or an answer that can be weighed.

My chaplain says a stranger bearing a scythe will come to free us. I have given him a room and a place at my table. Until the stranger arrives, I require the grain.

Nerys

*A clerk's endorsement: The enclosed coins were returned to the general chest. No reply was entered.*

Your objection is fair. A misaligned instrument proves only that someone should repair the instrument.

## sky-01 — A complaint about the observatory bells

Mara, baker at the lower stair · 4 Longrain, 642 · letter

Sage Selen,

Your novice says the dawn bell is late because the stars have moved. My dough has risen on time. I am inclined to trust the dough.

I do not object to the prayers. My sister went to your infirmary when the roof fell, and no one asked her what she believed before setting the bone. I object to six bells where there used to be one, and to being charged for their rope.

The ground has stood still through my father's life and mine. If you wish to call that kindness, call it kindness. Do not put the cost on my flour account without asking.

I enclose yesterday's rolls. Give the burned ones to the novice who argued with me. He will know why.

## sky-02 — Instructions for a bowl of water

Vara, surveyor at Stormspire · 12 Longrain, 642 · memorandum

For the Frostmere observer:

Use a shallow bowl on a stone shelf. Mark the waterline before the evening watch. Hang a plumb weight beside it. Keep the stove cold until the reading is entered. Warm air can move a thread more persuasively than any god.

Enter what you see before you speak with the other stations. Otherwise three witnesses become one witness with three signatures.

I am not asking you to stop believing in the carrier. I am asking you to stop using its name in the column headed measurement. There is a column for interpretation on the reverse. You may have all of it.

Keep the unremarkable nights as well. A book containing only wonders is an account with all the expenses torn out.

## sky-04 — An objection entered on the reverse

Vara to Selen · 20 Longrain, 642 · letter

Selen,

The agreement is interesting. It may mean the sky moves around us. It may mean our ground moves through the sky. It does not yet distinguish the two. Nor does movement require a living thing beneath the wheat.

I saw a dragon burn a relay roof last winter. Please do not write that I deny dragons. I deny that seeing one above a roof tells us what lies below a mountain.

You have left space for my answer, as promised. I appreciate that more than you will appreciate this answer.

I have kept Mara's complaint with the readings. Her oven is a poor astronomical instrument and an excellent warning against asking people to pay for a conclusion we have not established.

We removed the fallen lining at the ninth descent. Beneath the clay was a dark surface which did not take the chisel as stone does. The mason called it old timber until the lamp reached the next section. There was no grain.

I asked for a sample. He refused to strike it again. He had felt a slow warmth through the handle. I felt it with my bare palm. That is all I can attest.

## signal-04 — A watch in rain

Leth, second keeper at the high relay · 20 Longrain, 642 · witness

The assurance lamp burned for the western gate. Anja worked the shutter twice at each interval as ordered. Rain entered the copper repair and jammed it open.

From the stair the light looked steady. From Gannet it must have looked steady also. I told her. She put out the lamp.

We argued about it afterward, for the length of time it took to find dry tinder for the warning red. She said a wrong light was worse than none. I said none would be taken for a fallen gate. Both things could be true, which did not help us light the lamp.

The road below was already carrying people. I could hear cart wheels before I could see them.

## maker-02 — No household beneath another

Charter copy held at Titan’s Rest · Copy dated 2 Thaw, 618 · record

From the bronze charter, third panel:

No household shall be entered as ballast, burden, offering, or freight. No ruler may promise the lives of those dwelling beyond his road in exchange for the safety of his own wall.

Let every crown keep this covenant with the others, for what carries one hearth carries the next.

## maker-03 — An object no furnace would accept

Edda to Iven · 15 Longrain, 642 · letter

Iven,

They found this in the west seam after the attack. The miners called it a scale. I have seen fish scales and this is nothing like one, but I have no better word for a thing that came off the dragon's flank.

Ashenveil would not melt it. The factor said it softened in the heat and then set again with the same marks along its edge. He sent it back wrapped in my brother's last payroll sheet, which was an unkind choice of paper.

Can you tell whether the marks were cut? If it is only a piece of burned roof, tell me. I do not want another useful rumor. I want to know what killed the men whose names I keep having to send to the paymaster.

## maker-04 — Iven refuses a commission

Iven to a purchasing agent · 19 Longrain, 642 · letter

Sir,

The piece is not for sale. It belongs to the woman who asked me to examine it. My inability to afford iron does not make her property mine.

There are six identical hollows on its edge. A hammer does not make six identical mistakes. I tried the smallest punch I own on a broken tip. It left no mark. Please stop calling the hollows proof of a craftsman's work. They are a reason to ask a craftsman a question.

I will make you a hinge when the road opens. I will not make you a story in which the king's enemies are the only people who have ever touched a strange material.

I have returned your deposit. Count it. Everyone seems to count differently this month.

## maker-06 — Two surfaces that should not share a name

Hest’s comparison sheet · 7 Palesun, 642 · record

The broken piece sent by Edda is cold at room temperature. Its edge bears repeated hollows. When warmed, the fine fractures close unevenly and reopen on cooling. I have stopped the experiment to preserve what remains.

The lower-gallery surface is warm without a lamp, continuous beyond the exposed seam, and unmarked where visible. No sample taken.

Both have been called scale in the letters. This has caused people to conclude that what burned the mine must be the thing beneath the gallery. The same word is doing work the evidence has not done.

I have returned Edda's piece with the measurements. Please keep the surfaces distinct in any copies. There is no kindness in inventing a certainty for a grieving person and letting someone else remove it later.

## maker-07 — A request for the man who mends books

## maker-08 — The page behind the blue fish

Father said not to sell this one, even if we sold the press. I thought it was because he painted the fish for me.

There is a leaf behind the endpaper. It says the first child carried the hearths, and the second grew tired of being offered a place among them. He made creatures that would answer him without arguing. They had wings because he wished to be seen above his brother.

## maker-09 — A book-restorer asks for the margin

Pell, dictated through the laundry door · 23 Palesun, 642 · letter

To whoever has my daughter's book:

The border marks are counting marks from the old court. The black fragments carry them beneath the edge, repeated where separate plates would meet. I have copied three. Keep the copies apart until another reader has made their own.

The guards believe a word will command the creatures. I do not. A number tells you a maker counted something. It does not tell you his name, his purpose, or how to make the thing obey you.

The old leaf names the second child as maker. I have spent my life repairing what people wished their ancestors had written. This leaf is old. That is what I know. Compare it with the fragments before you ask me to know more.

If you find a margin in the next message, tell my daughter I remember the fish facing the wrong way. She was six. I have never corrected it.
