# Warscythe: one world, many witnesses

Editorial proposal, 21 November 2026. Planning only. Existing game files, lore, rewards and deployment remain unchanged. New minor characters, dates, trade routes, monetary names and events below are working proposals, not silently adopted canon.

## Canon supplied by the user

- Every region belongs to the same inhabited land upon one immense living dragon. The dragon travels through an astral, cosmic environment.
- The carrier deliberately holds a steady attitude and avoids movements that would endanger the people living upon it.
- Sages venerate the carrier. Many ordinary citizens believe they live on ordinary stationary land and dispute the carrier's existence.
- The earlier divine-family premise remains: the carrier is one child of the divine Emperor and Empress; its hostile sibling creates the invading dragons.
- History should emerge from connected letters, prison scraps, royal correspondence, prices, shortages and ordinary conversation. People have lives and concerns beyond a predicted liberator.
- A book or letter presentation should feel physical, with actual turning pages where appropriate. No implementation is authorized by this plan.

Working interpretation: citizens dispute the land-bearing dragon, not necessarily the smaller dragons attacking their settlements. Otherwise every witnessed attack would immediately settle the wrong argument. The manuscript should make this distinction explicit.

## The story's central tension

The same unshaken ground supports opposing interpretations. A baker sees stability as evidence that the sages invented a creature. A sage sees it as evidence of a creature's care. Neither needs to be foolish.

Citizens can see an extraordinary sky without knowing what lies beneath their ground. Most lives are spent far inland; familiar soil, rivers and stone do not announce what carries them. Do not solve the disagreement by making skeptics deny something plainly visible from every doorway. The precise horizon/edge geography must remain consistent once chosen.

Author-only truth: the carrier is real and remains steady by choice. Its refusal to roll or make a sudden evasive movement protects its inhabitants even when its sibling's creatures threaten them. This creates a meaningful limit on its power. Do not later solve the war by having it casually shake the inhabitants or invaders off its back.

Reader knowledge must be narrower. The opening should no longer announce the dragon as an uncontested fact, as the previous onboarding draft did. Attribute the claim: the sages say the world is carried. Let readers discover why that claim is disputed, useful, exploited or eventually supported.

The origin of the hostile dragons is another author-level truth. Conflicting local birth stories can survive as legends, propaganda or incomplete observations; they cannot all remain equally authoritative accounts of unrelated universes. The earlier repeated elemental-birth paragraphs need an editorial replacement pass, not a blanket excuse that every continuity error is an unreliable narrator.

## Groups with reasons, not costumes

| Group | What they want | What they know or get wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical skeptics | Food, safe roads, reasonable taxes and evidence | Familiar stone and still water support their ordinary-land model. Skepticism does not make them immoral. |
| Devout sages | Preserve observatories, rituals, records and an obligation to the carrier | Some have real observations; some inherit faith; some admit uncertainty. Their prayers need not be a mechanism that physically pilots the dragon. |
| Civic and royal courts | Defend territory, maintain legitimacy and distribute scarce resources | Some act honorably, some hide failures, and some make defensible decisions with terrible consequences. |
| Occupation officials and collaborators | Keep authority, supplies and useful prisoners | Their paperwork can connect invasions across borders without revealing every supernatural secret. |
| Ordinary tradespeople and families | Keep someone alive and a business open | They reveal war through debt, missing deliveries, mended clothing and changed habits. They do not exist merely to praise the protagonist. |

The sages can include scholars, opportunists and sincere doubters. The skeptics can include honest investigators and demagogues. Do not turn the faith dispute into good believers versus stupid civilians.

## Continuity problem already present

The live source has competing regional names. src/store/constants.js and MapSection use The Threshold, Ashwood Frontier, The Crucible, Ironveil Fortress and further names. The ten-region art/chronicle sources use The Threshold, Ashenveil, Frostmere, Shadowfen, The Oasis, Aurelius Keep, Bonehollow, Stormspire, The Abyss and Titan's Rest. The region-two asset bundle itself labels Ashwood Frontier while including an Ashenveil soundscape and lava-dragon assets.

Recommendation: establish one master gazetteer before commissioning a full manuscript or making data changes. The ten established art/Empress geographies are the working province names in this proposal because their art, guardians and dragon identities already form a coherent set. The other names need an approved decision: retained provinces, named districts/landmarks, or campaign-stage labels. Do not silently move them between categories in production.

Existing named Empresses and their identities remain: Liora, Vessa, Sera, Nyxara, Amara, Auren, Isolde, Kaira, Thalassia and Regia. They are not automatically the mother, daughters or spouses of any newly introduced mortal royal. The divine Empress who is parent to the two children remains a separate identity. The Fitness deities receive no invented genealogy in this pass.

## A shared geography and economy

These proposed economic roles connect existing environments. They are not claims that the current app has a simulated market.

| Province, using art/chronicle names | Everyday contribution | Dependencies that create stories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Threshold | Grain, market yards, the first major gate and courier exchanges | Needs imported metal and functioning roads; price changes reach ordinary households here first |
| Ashenveil | Smelting, worked iron and furnace tools | Needs ore, food and transport; attacks close furnaces or routes, making repairs and weapons expensive elsewhere |
| Frostmere | Preserved food, cold-storage knowledge and observatories | Needs fuel and replacement metal; its sky records can be compared with distant observers |
| Shadowfen | Causeways, river transport and medicinal gathering | Needs structural timber and iron; occupation can seize both trade and correspondence |
| The Oasis | Cisterns, caravan staging, resins and scarce water | Needs tools and reliable payments; accepts outsiders' money only while the shared system is trusted |
| Aurelius Keep | Courts, archives, a major mint and official dispatches | Needs supplies from the provinces; royal decisions have consequences far from the palace |
| Bonehollow | Older mines, salvage and burial records | Supplies material to Ashenveil; a dead worker's payroll can contradict an official claim that a mine is functioning |
| Stormspire | Signal towers, relay stations and storm-resistant fittings | Needs food and repair crews; missing signals interrupt more than one kingdom's defensive plans |
| The Abyss | Dangerous deep surveys and rare observational materials | Needs surface rescue routes and supplies; its records can distinguish belief from physical evidence |
| Titan's Rest | Old civic charters, bronze works and veterans' stores | Depends on living trade despite claims of self-sufficiency; preserves earlier versions of the same treaties |

All are places on one landscape. Couriers travel by roads, boats, bridges and caravans. Distinct weather and architecture do not imply distinct planets. Cosmic travel is outside this shared geography. Detailed gravity, horizons and the astral light cycle remain author-worldbuilding decisions to settle consistently before writing scenes that depend on them.

Example causal chain: a route through Ashenveil closes; worked iron stops arriving; smiths raise prices; farmers postpone repairs; grain transport slows; a distant garrison receives less food; a court debases or borrows money; another court refuses those coins; its requested relief arrives late. Each link can appear in a different kind of document.

Use one shared calendar, measures and monetary vocabulary, with local variations recorded explicitly. For the pilot, silver marks are a proposed fiction-only unit; prices are not actual app shop prices or a justification for real purchases. Dates below are editorial days relative to the Gannet Causeway evacuation, not an approved new in-world calendar.

## The first connected story: The Chain That Never Arrived

Working locations and new minor figures:

- Princess Maelin: a mortal court envoy at Aurelius Keep. Her relationship to existing rulers/Empress Auren is deliberately unassigned.
- Ser Corren: a courier-captain holding a refugee crossing near the Threshold/Shadowfen trade corridor.
- Iven: a skeptical Threshold smith. His family and customers experience war through prices rather than prophecy.
- Minister Deren: a treasury official who diverts supplies to defend the capital. He has reasons, responsibility and something to conceal.
- Neral: a way-clerk captured at the crossing. He copied the cargo changes before his arrest.
- Sage Selen: an observer at Frostmere, writing privately as well as institutionally.
- Queen Nerys: a proposed mortal ruler associated with the Oasis court. She is not Empress Amara under another name.

Single incident: Maelin promises three hundred lengths of chain so Corren can maintain a refugee crossing. The same chain is diverted to the capital's western defenses. A copied authorization and a missing reply later expose the decision. Corren's record is initially reduced to an accusation of abandonment. His own last letter tells a different story.

The chain is iron, public trust, correspondence and the roads between regions at once. It should remain a physical object people needed, not an explained metaphor in every passage.

Readers can also find these out of order. Each piece must still make local sense. Do not make core app use depend on solving this story.

## How the wider plot grows

Thread A, the missing chain, establishes material consequences and the human cost of a royal decision. Thread B, the still ground, follows observations, prayer, jokes, suppressed reports and disagreements about the carrier. Thread C, the makers' marks, gradually links apparently different invading beasts to one source. Thread D, the expected liberator, includes hope, fraud, political recruitment and people who care more about a reopened road than the hero's identity.

These threads cross but are not identical. A smith's account does not need to mention destiny. A prisoner's damaged scrap should not suddenly contain an omniscient cosmology. Royal correspondents can be wrong about distant falls and correct about the weight of a silver payment.

A queen might address a neighboring king, a princess might commission a knight, and a quartermaster might leave an angry correction on the same dispatch. Royal titles and family relationships need a short office/kinship register before these become final names. Letters should make political relationships clear without implying that the regions occupy separate worlds.

## Document and voice rules

- Every document has a reason to exist before the player finds it: procure food, keep a promise, conceal an order, request help, say goodbye, collect a debt or make someone laugh.
- Writers know only what their role, location and date permit. Record the author-level truth separately from each claim.
- Give people different diction. A smith quotes costs; a minister uses precise euphemisms; a frightened clerk repeats a number; a princess notices an unanswered phrase. Let some people be blunt or funny.
- Keep a generous portion of ordinary life. Not every scrap is a puzzle, prophecy or revelation. Some make the connected places inhabitable.
- Recur through physical particulars: shipment 27, a blue sealing cord, repaired boots, a disputed measure of silver. Do not rely only on repeated names.
- A contradiction needs a cause: timing, hearsay, censorship, propaganda, mistake, mistranslation or a deliberate lie. Unreliability is not permission to lose track of facts.
- Use damage sparingly and honestly. A missing line can hide a recipient or order; do not tear away every important noun merely to manufacture mystery. Provide a legible transcription.
- Avoid universal heroic narration and app vocabulary. Citizens write about harvest, wages and the people they miss. They do not explain experience points, subscriptions or onboarding to one another.
- Preserve spoilers. A pre-liberation letter cannot congratulate a victory that has not occurred. An archive note written later needs its own date and attribution.

## Production record for every piece

Maintain a simple editorial index: document ID; title as the reader sees it; form/material; author/recipient and their relationship; written date; origin/destination/found location; what the author believes; what actually happened; practical purpose; links to other documents; discovery prerequisites; forbidden spoilers; reading length; and what physical damage is meaningful.

Track routes, missing shipments, injuries, deaths, imprisonment and who has received which letter on one master chronology. A linked story should remain consistent if the reader collects the evidence in a different order.

Discovery can feel incidental while being editorially controlled. Guarantee essential connections through normal progression; optional flavor can be less predictable. Distinguish when a letter was written, when it was delivered, and when the player recovered it. A page number is not a discovery entitlement, and turning a book page cannot unlock an unearned document.

## Book and letter presentation brief

Collected documents become a codex whose pages can actually turn. Native selectable text stays separate from parchment/art. A two-page spread suits wider screens; a phone needs a readable single page and reliable tap controls. A letter opens/unfolds; a bound codex turns. Do not apply the same elaborate animation to every receipt or scrap.

Keep the physical original and an optional clear transcription. The reader can inspect a seal, reverse a sheet or see a damaged edge without being forced to decode decorative handwriting. A cross-reference can offer another already-found document without spoiling an unfound sender's identity. Provide chronological and collection order as optional reading aids.

The visual effect must preserve text reading, current location, keyboard controls, reduced motion, narrow-screen layout and interruption/resume. No pages should contain baked-in raster text. Research of implementation candidates follows this editorial plan; no package is installed by this task.

## Next editorial work and acceptance checks

First resolve the gazetteer and title hierarchy. Review the pilot's voices and causal chain before expanding to a complete document corpus. Then map the approved material to discoveries and outline the book interaction. Write additional regional documents only when they connect to the master chronology or a genuine standalone life.

Acceptance questions: Can a reader explain why a spear costs more without a narrator lecturing them? Do two regions visibly rely on each other? Does the dead knight's letter change a royal letter's meaning? Are the skeptical and faithful positions both intelligible? Does each author know only what they could know? Does the carrier remain consistent with the user's steady, protective dragon? Is the same world still coherent when documents are found out of order?

Open decisions: official region names; mortal royal offices versus Empress keepers; common calendar/currency; the limits of sky observation and travel to the world's edge; when readers receive decisive proof of the carrier; how the existing fairy-origin accounts relate to the confirmed divine-family premise. None has been silently changed in the app.

The larger plot also needs an eventual answer to why the divine parents do not simply stop their hostile child. Do not invent that answer in a casual letter before its author-level rule is settled. Public theological arguments may disagree while the writer keeps the unresolved question visible.
